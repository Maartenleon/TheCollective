# Symptoms: what leadership teams recognise

The twenty symptoms we work from, eight of which are on the site, with who feels each one most, where it starts in the framework, the cause, the concepts people search for, and the evidence that it is common.

Use this file as the reference when you edit them in `index.html`, write landing pages or articles, or pick statements for a deck or LinkedIn post. Statistics are here for our own confidence and for longer articles; the site itself does not quote them yet.

**How to quote the evidence.** Several sources are older (Booz & Company 2011, Sull 2015, McKinsey 2014) or are vendor figures or forecasts (Planview, Zylo, Flexera; Gartner's ERP and AI predictions). Present them as "research shows", not as this year's numbers. Most samples are large, US-heavy companies; there is no Benelux-specific data. Avoid the popular "15% flow efficiency" figure: it has no solid evidence behind it.

## Plan · the piece

*Are we working on the right things?*

### 1. "We have 40 initiatives running, and nobody can tell me which three actually matter."
- **Anchor:** `#too-many-initiatives`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, COO
- **Cause:** The strategy names ambitions but makes no choices. Every idea becomes a project, and nothing is ranked against anything else.
- **Concepts (tags):** Strategic prioritisation, Project portfolio management, OKRs
- **Evidence:** 64% of 1,800+ executives name too many conflicting priorities as their biggest frustration (Booz & Company / Strategy&, 2011). Over 60% of 1,364 C-suite executives chose seven or more of ten priorities as current focus (Deloitte, 2022).

### 2. "The strategy is clear in the boardroom. Two levels down, nobody can tell you what it means for their work."
- **Anchor:** `#strategy-lost-in-translation`
- **Felt most by:** CEO
- **Cause:** The plan goes straight to the organisation without a design in between: which capabilities, which teams, which owners.
- **Concepts (tags):** Strategy execution, Strategic alignment, Operating model
- **Evidence:** 74% of executives worry their strategy has not been translated into tangible actions (Strategy&). Only half of middle managers can name any of their company's top five priorities (Sull et al., MIT/HBR).

### 3. "Everyone wants AI, but our data and systems aren't ready for it."
- **Anchor:** `#ai-without-foundations`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, CIO, CTO
- **Cause:** The ambition is set at the top, but the foundations it depends on, data, process ownership and capacity, were never planned or funded.
- **Concepts (tags):** AI readiness, AI strategy, Data foundations
- **Evidence:** Gartner predicts that through 2026 organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects not supported by AI-ready data (forecast, 2025).


## Blueprint · the notes

*Is it clear who owns what?*

### 4. "Every piece of work needs three other teams, and we're always waiting on someone."
- **Anchor:** `#waiting-on-other-teams`
- **Felt most by:** CIO, CTO, COO
- **Cause:** Teams are organised around departments or systems instead of the flow of work to the customer, so every change crosses several boundaries.
- **Concepts (tags):** Team dependencies, Value streams, Team Topologies
- **Evidence:** 87% are slowed by dependencies or handoffs (Planview, 2023). Only 9% of managers can always rely on colleagues in other functions (Sull et al., MIT/HBR).

### 5. "We went agile two years ago. We're not delivering any faster."
- **Anchor:** `#agile-but-not-faster`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, CIO, CTO
- **Cause:** The teams changed how they work, but funding, priorities and dependencies above the teams stayed the same. Fast teams still wait on a slow system.
- **Concepts (tags):** Agile at scale, Agile transformation, Lean portfolio management
- **Evidence:** Satisfaction with agile fell from 71% to 59% in a year; only 44% say agile works well across the organisation (Digital.ai, 17th State of Agile).

### 6. "We reorganised, and a year later we have the same problems."
- **Anchor:** `#reorganised-same-problems`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, COO
- **Cause:** The org chart changed, but decision rights, processes and the way work flows did not.
- **Concepts (tags):** Organisational design, Reorganisation, Operating model design
- **Evidence:** Only 23% of executives said their reorganisation met its objectives and improved performance (McKinsey, 2014); a 2025 McKinsey survey on operating-model redesign is more positive (63%).

### 7. "When an order goes wrong between sales, the warehouse and finance, nobody owns the whole thing."
- **Anchor:** `#nobody-owns-end-to-end`
- **Felt most by:** COO, CFO
- **Cause:** Each department owns its own piece. Nobody owns the end-to-end flow, such as order-to-cash, returns or replenishment.
- **Concepts (tags):** End-to-end process ownership, Order-to-cash, Value streams
- **Evidence:** Managers are three times more likely to miss commitments because other units don't support them than because of their own team (Sull et al., MIT/HBR).

### 8. "We have three reports with three different numbers, and a meeting to argue about which one is right."
- **Anchor:** `#three-versions-of-the-truth`
- **Felt most by:** CFO, CEO
- **Cause:** Nobody owns the key definitions and data, so every department builds its own version of the truth.
- **Concepts (tags):** Data governance, Single source of truth, Data ownership
- **Evidence:** Gartner estimates poor data quality costs organisations an average of $12.9 million a year (indicative, 2020).

### 9. "IT is seen as a cost to manage, not as part of how we compete."
- **Anchor:** `#it-as-a-cost-centre`
- **Felt most by:** CIO, CTO, CEO
- **Cause:** Business and IT plan separately and hand work over the wall, instead of owning the outcome together.
- **Concepts (tags):** Business–IT alignment, Product operating model, Digital operating model
- **Evidence:** Where business and IT jointly own digital delivery, 71% of targets are met against 48% on average (Gartner, 2025).

### 21. "We merged two years ago, and we still run two of everything."
- **Anchor:** `#two-of-everything`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, COO, CIO
- **Cause:** The deal was integrated on paper: one name, one board. Nobody decided which processes, systems and teams become shared and which stay apart, so both keep running.
- **Concepts (tags):** Post-merger integration, Operating model, Shared services
- **Evidence:** Still to add. Draft line, not yet researched.


## Means · the orchestra

*Do we have the people and money to do it?*

### 10. "Our costs keep creeping up, especially IT, and nobody can tell me exactly why."
- **Anchor:** `#costs-creeping-up`
- **Felt most by:** CFO, CEO
- **Cause:** Spend is spread across cloud, licences and teams that nobody oversees as a whole. New things are added, old ones are rarely switched off.
- **Concepts (tags):** IT cost optimisation, OPEX, Cloud costs
- **Evidence:** Average tech budgets rose from 4.25% to 5.49% of revenue between 2020 and 2023 (Deloitte). Cloud budgets are overrun by 17% and an estimated 27% of cloud spend is wasted (Flexera, 2025).

### 11. "Most of our IT budget goes to keeping things running. There's very little left for anything new."
- **Anchor:** `#run-eats-the-budget`
- **Felt most by:** CIO, CTO, CFO
- **Cause:** Old systems and quick fixes build up a maintenance bill that grows every year and crowds out new work.
- **Concepts (tags):** Technical debt, Run versus change, IT budget
- **Evidence:** 55% of the tech budget goes to existing operations and 19% to new capabilities (Deloitte CIO surveys). CIOs say 10–20% of new-product budget is diverted to technical debt (McKinsey).

### 12. "The same handful of people are needed on every project, and they're also the ones keeping the business running."
- **Anchor:** `#same-people-everywhere`
- **Felt most by:** COO, CIO, CTO
- **Cause:** Projects are approved without checking who will actually do the work, so a few key people become the bottleneck for everything.
- **Concepts (tags):** Capacity planning, Resource management, Key-person dependency
- **Evidence:** 55% of value streams have twice as much work as they can handle (Planview, 2023). Over 60% link large-programme failure to a missing master plan, including realistic resourcing (BCG, 2024).

### 13. "Every department buys its own software, and nobody has the full picture of what we have or what we pay for."
- **Anchor:** `#software-sprawl`
- **Felt most by:** CIO, CTO, CFO
- **Cause:** Buying decisions are spread across departments, with no shared view of the capabilities the organisation already has.
- **Concepts (tags):** SaaS sprawl, Shadow IT, Application portfolio
- **Evidence:** The average company runs 275 SaaS applications; business units control 70% of SaaS spend (Zylo, 2025).


## Orchestration · the conductor

*Do we decide, stop and adjust in time?*

### 14. "We can't tell whether our projects actually pay off."
- **Anchor:** `#projects-dont-pay-off`
- **Felt most by:** CFO, CEO
- **Cause:** Projects are approved on a business case, but nobody owns measuring the benefits once the money is spent.
- **Concepts (tags):** Return on investment, Business case, Value management
- **Evidence:** Only 48% of digital initiatives meet or exceed their business targets (Gartner, 2025 CIO survey, 3,186 CIOs).

### 15. "Too many decisions end up on my desk, and they still take too long."
- **Anchor:** `#decisions-escalate`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, COO
- **Cause:** Nobody has made explicit who decides what, so managers escalate instead of settling trade-offs between themselves.
- **Concepts (tags):** Decision rights, Governance, RACI
- **Evidence:** Only 20% of organisations excel at decision making; 61% say at least half their decision time is used ineffectively (McKinsey). CEOs rate 35% of decision-meeting time as inefficient (PwC CEO Survey).

### 16. "Every project report says green, but the numbers that matter don't move."
- **Anchor:** `#everything-reports-green`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, CFO
- **Cause:** Reports track activity and milestones rather than results, and bad news travels up slowly.
- **Concepts (tags):** Watermelon reporting, Portfolio reporting, Outcome KPIs
- **Evidence:** Project status reports are biased 60% of the time, and the bias is more than twice as likely to be optimistic (Keil et al., MIT Sloan Management Review). Only 7% of executives know exactly how each team's work supports the biggest goals (Atlassian, 2025).

### 17. "Our budget looks like last year's, even though our priorities have changed."
- **Anchor:** `#budget-like-last-year`
- **Felt most by:** CFO, CEO
- **Cause:** The annual budget starts from last year's numbers, so money and people stay where they were, whatever the strategy says.
- **Concepts (tags):** Resource allocation, Zero-based budgeting, Rolling forecasts
- **Evidence:** 90% of the dollars stay where they were the year before (McKinsey, 1,500 companies over 20 years). About half of CEOs reallocate 10% or less of resources each year (PwC, 28th CEO Survey).

### 18. "We're good at starting things. We almost never stop anything."
- **Anchor:** `#never-stop-anything`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, CFO, COO
- **Cause:** There is no regular moment where the portfolio is reviewed and work is stopped, so not deciding becomes the decision.
- **Concepts (tags):** Portfolio governance, Stage gates, Work in progress
- **Evidence:** Constant reprioritisation leads to about 40% wasted effort (Planview, 2023).

### 19. "The new system went live, but the benefits in the business case never showed up."
- **Anchor:** `#benefits-never-showed-up`
- **Felt most by:** CFO, COO
- **Cause:** Success was defined as go-live. The programme team moved on, and nobody in the business owns the promised outcome.
- **Concepts (tags):** Benefits realisation, ERP implementation, Post-implementation review
- **Evidence:** Gartner predicts more than 70% of recent ERP initiatives will not fully meet their original business-case goals by 2027 (forecast).

### 20. "Our big programme is late, over budget, and still getting bigger."
- **Anchor:** `#programme-keeps-growing`
- **Felt most by:** CEO, CFO, CIO
- **Cause:** Scope, dependencies and capacity were never planned end to end, and business and IT don't share ownership.
- **Concepts (tags):** Programme recovery, Programme management, Transformation
- **Evidence:** Only 30% of large tech programmes fully meet expectations on time, budget and scope (BCG, 2024, 1,000+ companies).
