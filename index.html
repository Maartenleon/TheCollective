The framework

One whole, four parts.

Those symptoms share a cause. One of the parts below is missing, or the parts don’t connect.

Plan

What we want to play

The plan holds the ambition, the choices and the goals. Most organisations have one, and it is usually good. Its weak point is the handover: it goes straight to the people who must carry it out, as a list of projects. A plan works when every goal lands somewhere it can be measured.

  • Strategy
  • OKRs

Blueprint

What it takes to play it

The blueprint is the intended design, on paper. It shows which capabilities the organisation needs, how value reaches the customer, how teams are shaped and who decides what. It is the only route from intending to doing. Skip it, and everyone plays the part they already knew.

  • Value streams
  • Business capabilities
  • Operating model
  • Organisational design

Means

Who is in the chair tonight

People, money, processes and technology. They are finite, and both run and change draw on them. Tonight’s concert and next season’s rehearsals use the same musicians and the same hours. That is why the two so often compete instead of cooperate.

  • People
  • Process
  • Technology
  • Spend

Orchestration

The whole, in motion

The plan, the blueprint and the means running together, day to day. Orchestration is the rhythm of decisions that keeps them in step: what starts, what stops, and who gets the people when run and change collide. This is where we work, until the orchestra plays on its own.

  • Portfolio management
